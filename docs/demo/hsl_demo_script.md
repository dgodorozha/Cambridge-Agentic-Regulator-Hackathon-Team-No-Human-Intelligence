# HSL demonstration: narration with timestamps

**0:00**  Meet the Herding Scenario Lab.

**0:05**  Where a regulator finds out, before the next crash, whether its surveillance tools and circuit breakers actually work.

**0:16**  Trading agents built on the same models can herd. A small shock becomes a crash.

**0:23**  Today a surveillance tool is judged on how well its signal mimics the market. That is not the question a supervisor has to answer.

**0:30**  The tool that looks best can be the worst at spotting the agents that cause the crash. Nobody measures that gap today.

**0:36**  Here is how the Lab gets an answer a regulator can rely on. It builds markets where the destabilising agents are known by construction, and checks that against which agents actually moved prices. It scores every tool on whether it found those agents, and every rule on how much of the crash it prevented. False alerts carry a finite sample guarantee. And it attacks its own answer: reshuffled scenarios, unseen ones, an adversary hiding, and a critic recomputing every figure before a named human releases it.

**1:01**  Every run begins with the supervisor's question: would a targeted throttle have contained a flash crash with forty per cent of the market on one vendor's model, and which tool deserves certification?

**1:10**  Your own data next: a synthetic sample of a venue's order flow, through a quarantine before it is stored.

**1:15**  Accepted, fingerprinted, classified, and used to calibrate the markets to yours.

**1:23**  A file carrying an email address is refused outright. Personal data never enters the sandbox.

**1:30**  Plan. The Lab builds a battery of markets: strong herds, an evader, colluding agents, a faulty vendor model, and the exact case asked about, all hashed.

**1:39**  Human in the loop: nothing runs without a named approver on the regulator's register. This name is not on it: refused, and recorded.

**1:45**  R. Ahmed is on the register as an SMF24. He approves the battery, the approval is sealed into the ledger, and the run begins.

**1:52**  Watch the markets trade: ordinary traders, liquidity providers, agents on shared AI models, and one cluster trying to ignite momentum. Eight surveillance tools score every market.

**2:02**  The market as a network. Squares are the agents that really caused the crash; amber rings are what the tool flagged; red rings, the ones it missed.

**2:10**  Before anyone can release a result, an independent critic recomputes every figure from the raw data and replays the audit trail.

**2:17**  Every step, approval and refusal is a signed, hash chained ledger entry a third party can replay; seventeen security controls are checked live, and a failure blocks release.

**2:26**  The finding: by how well it mimics market statistics, the volatility trigger comes first; by whether it catches the agents who cause the crash, it comes last. That is the decision gap.

**2:36**  Gate two: the registered approver releases the briefing: the answer to the question asked, the certified tool and its guarantee, every rule scored, and an evidence pack with a transparency record.

**2:46**  And every other page of the same signed run.

**2:48**  Herding Scenario Lab, from No Human Intelligence. Certify the tool. Contain the crash. Keep the human in charge.
